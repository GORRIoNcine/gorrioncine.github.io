Projects / Fangal

Fangal

Fiction · Feature · 2030 · 80 min (previsto) · Development

Silence kills, too.

Maret, a young filmmaker, wields her camera as an extension of her memory to capture the pulse of “Yonse,” a remote town she visits with her boyfriend Marcos before their paths diverge. Through her experiences, she paints a raw portrait of a family with secrets and violent customs.

Maret, a young filmmaker, travels with her boyfriend Marcos to “Yonse,” a remote village in eastern Cuba, where he has spent his vacations since childhood. It is their last time together before Marcos leaves for mandatory military service. Maret brings her camera to document those final days together.

Marcos’s family lives in a complex of courtyards connecting wooden houses. They are welcomed by Eri, a woman who cares for her mother, Nancy—an elderly woman with dementia who spends her days motionless under a thatched shelter. Next door, Maret meets Mili, the wife of Chichi, the head of the family, and Beto, Nancy’s ex-husband and the father of Eri and Chichi. Marcos takes Maret on a tour to visit the rest of their distant relatives, and she is fascinated by the surroundings.

Maret’s first shock comes on the first day, when a horseman brings a dead sheep and a tied-up dog that he accuses of being the killer. Maret takes a photo moments before the dog’s head is severed by an axe blow right before her eyes. That night, she realizes that the camera not only records but also reveals something that beats beneath the surface of memory. She decides to shoot a documentary, which becomes a dissection of the landscape and those who inhabit it.

On the second day, while filming Nancy, Beto approaches her, causing the elderly woman to burst into screams, accusing him of having killed someone. Maret interviews Mili, who tells her that Nancy’s dementia flared up on the very same day that La China’s daughter—a neighbor who lives alone—disappeared from the beach. That day, Nancy had a violent outburst, and the girl never returned from the sea. Maret and Marcos stay at La China’s house, where she keeps her daughter’s memory alive in an untouched quinceañera-themed room, and shows them the photos she keeps as if on an altar.

In an interview for the documentary, Marcos recounts that as a child he saw Beto inside the barn with a woman who was screaming. Eri reveals that Nancy caught Beto cheating on her his entire life, and on the day at the beach, she wouldn’t stop accusing Beto of murdering La China’s daughter, but no one believed her.

On the last day of the trip, they go on an outing to the beach. Maret ends up drinking too much after Beto gives her a bottle of rum. On the way back, they seat her—dizzy—next to Nancy. The old woman whispers to her that she is dead and describes Beto drowning her. That night, Maret dreams that she is reincarnated as La China’s daughter, being drowned by Beto, while Nancy tries to save her. Then she is reincarnated as Nancy, and just as she manages to get close, she sees herself drowning in the sea. As she says goodbye to the family, she asks to take one last portrait. Everyone gathers around Nancy for the photo. Maret and Marcos leave in a cart. He picks up the camera to ask Maret how she’s been. She looks into the lens; her gaze still holds the mud and the certainty that there are deaths that do not drown in the sea but in the silence of those around her.
